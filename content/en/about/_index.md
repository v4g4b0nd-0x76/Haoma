Haoma is a hobby podcast and open wiki about the myths of Iran and the wider East, built on primary sources.

The name is the Avestan word for the sacred plant pressed in the Yasna ritual, and for the divinity praised in the Hom Yasht (Yasna 9 to 11). Its Vedic cousin is Soma. We borrow it for what the drink stood for in the texts: renewal, healing and the memory of stories.

Everything is open source. Corrections are welcome through GitHub issues and pull requests. Every page lists its sources and is graded: primary text, later compilation, archaeology or modern scholarship.

## Using Haoma (researchers & authors)

Short path for reading and citing — no account needed.

- **Find a name** — use search (⌕) or the [A–Z]({{< relref "/az/" >}}) index; browse by idea on [Themes]({{< relref "/themes/" >}}).
- **Trust the grade** — the chip on each page says how close the claim sits to a primary text; full citations sit under **Sources**.
- **Cite the page** — **Cite this page** copies a short line (title, access date, URL).
- **Compare traditions** — [Compare]({{< relref "/compare/" >}}) sets two related figures side by side and names the differences.
- **Follow the atlas** — open a culture hub (for example [Iranian]({{< relref "/cultures/iranian/" >}})): the atlas is a map of how characters, stories and history link.
- **Export or download** — **Export PDF** on article and episode pages; [Library]({{< relref "/library/" >}}) holds downloadable PDFs, images and EPUBs.
- **Switch language** — English ↔ فارسی from the site header. Paths match across languages.
