<p class="lead">Japanese myth enters continuous writing through two early-Nara court projects: the <em>Kojiki</em> and the <em>Nihon Shoki</em>. They are not “folk scrapbooks” — they are imperial memory machines.</p>

## Two books, one age

Commissioned to organise genealogy and precedent, the chronicles preserve creation, kami wars, and the descent of rule. Where they diverge, Haoma prefers naming both rather than inventing a single “authentic” Japan.

{{< recon >}}
Nineteenth- and twentieth-century nationalist readings often treated these texts as timeless essence. Use them as dated court compilations with older layers — not as proof of one unbroken folk voice.
{{< /recon >}}
