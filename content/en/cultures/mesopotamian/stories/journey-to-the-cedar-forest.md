<p class="lead">In Tablet V the heroes set out to cut cedar in the forests beyond the western mountains, where the demon Huwawa guards the trees. This page follows their road across the map — the geography is real, the story is myth.</p>

The map description, stop by stop:

1. **Uruk (the starting point, southern Iraq).** Gilgamesh sets out from his own city with Enkidu and the hatchet, and with oxen to carry the timber. He prays to the sun god Shamash on the way out.
2. **Up the Euphrates.** The road of the epic is a real road: the Euphrates valley was the highway north. The heroes camp where the river is crossed, and Gilgamesh scares off the wolves with fire, which is why, the poem says, the desert wolves still fear fire.
3. **Carchemish (the turn west, on the Turkish-Syrian border today).** There the river route ends and the road climbs into the hills of the Amanus range.
4. **The passes.** Six miles of dark gorge, where the tallest cedar cannot fall without shaking the whole slope — so the text says of the forest itself.
5. **The cedar forest (the mountains of Lebanon and the Amanus).** The trees are sacred: reserved by the gods for Marduk's temple. Huwawa, appointed guardian of the forest by Enlil, lives there; the heroes bring him down, cut the timbers, and float them downriver to Eridu.
