<p class="lead">Enkidu is Gilgamesh’s equal and friend — formed in the wild, brought into the city, then bound to the king by combat and love.</p>

## From steppe to Uruk

The gods shape Enkidu from clay to check Gilgamesh’s arrogance. He lives with animals until Shamhat draws him toward human bread, beer and clothing. In Uruk he wrestles the king; neither wins alone, and friendship follows.

## The cedar road

Together they walk to Huwawa’s forest. See [The Cedar Forest](/cultures/mesopotamian/stories/journey-to-the-cedar-forest/) for the geography of that journey.

## Death and grief

The gods decree Enkidu’s death after the heroes offend divine order. Gilgamesh’s mourning opens the epic’s second half: the search for a life that will not end. Enkidu’s arc is the hinge between wild strength and human mortality.
