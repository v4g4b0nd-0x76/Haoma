<p class="lead">Odin is the chief of the Aesir, associated with wisdom, poetry, war and death. The poems that preserve him were written down in medieval Iceland; they are not a single ancient “bible” of the North.</p>

## Sacrifice for wisdom

He hung on the world tree Yggdrasil for nine nights, wounded by his own spear, to learn the runes. The prize is knowledge bought with pain — a pattern that runs through several Odin stories (the eye at Mímir’s well is another).

{{< recon >}}
Diagrams of Yggdrasil with nine labelled worlds are **modern teaching maps**. The Eddas name the tree and several realms; they do not publish one fixed floor-plan. Treat atlas-style cosmos charts as reconstruction, not a primary diagram.
{{< /recon >}}

## Names and roles

Poems call him by many names: Allfather, Wanderer, god of the hanged. He gathers the slain to Valhalla, seeks knowledge from seeresses, and still cannot avert the fate sung in [Ragnarok](/cultures/norse/stories/ragnarok/).

## Sources

The *Poetic Edda* (especially *Völuspá* and *Hávamál*) and Snorri’s *Prose Edda* are the main literary witnesses. Archaeology and place-names add context; they do not replace the poems.
