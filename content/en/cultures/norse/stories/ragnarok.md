<p class="lead">Ragnarok is the prophesied final battle in which many gods die and the world is remade. The seeress of <em>Völuspá</em> sings it as fate already known — not a surprise ending.</p>

## What the poem says

Signs gather: kin-slaughter, Fimbulwinter, the wolf and the serpent loosed. Gods and monsters meet on the field; [Odin](/cultures/norse/characters/odin/) falls to Fenrir, Thor to the Midgard Serpent. Fire and flood clear the old order. Then earth rises again, green, and a few survivors continue.

{{< recon >}}
Popular timelines that schedule Ragnarok like a movie beat-sheet, or that pin every creature to one “official” map of the nine worlds, are modern storytelling. Stick to the poem’s images first; use charts only as labelled reconstruction.

## After the fire

*Völuspá* ends with hope as well as ruin: a new hall, a renewed earth, the gods’ game-pieces found in the grass. Later prose (Snorri) fills gaps; where poem and prose disagree, prefer naming both.
