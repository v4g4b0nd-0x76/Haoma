<p class="lead">Mehr, Avestan <em>Miθra</em>, is the yazata (divine being worthy of worship) who guards covenants, truth and the light of day.</p>

## Name and meaning

The Avestan word *miθra* means "contract" or "agreement". The god is the personification of that bond: breaking an oath offends him directly, without mediation.

## In the Mihr Yasht

The hymn to Mehr (Yasht 10) describes a watchful, ever-awake lord with a thousand ears and ten thousand eyes. He rides ahead of the sun, sees every liar, and punishes those who break their word. He rewards honest dealing and protects the righteous who call on him.

> A world without trust cannot stand, so the guardian of trust never sleeps.
>
> — Mihr Yasht (paraphrase; exact wording varies across translations)

## Solar and cosmic role

Mehr drives his chariot across the sky ahead of the sun, dispelling darkness. He is linked to light but is not the sun god; Hvare-khshaeta ("the shining sun") fills that role. Mehr's domain is the covenant that makes social and cosmic order possible.

## Mehregan festival

The seventh month of the Iranian calendar is named Mehr, and its sixteenth day is celebrated as **Mehregan** — an autumn festival of harvest, gratitude and friendship that has been observed continuously for millennia and is still celebrated today.

## Relation to Roman Mithras

The Roman mystery cult of Mithras shares the name and some imagery (the bull-slaying *tauroctony*, solar symbolism), but scholars disagree about how much of Mehr's character carried over and how much was reinterpreted in a Hellenistic context. The two traditions are related by name and origin, not identical religions.
