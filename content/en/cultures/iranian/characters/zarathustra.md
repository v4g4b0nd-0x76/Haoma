<p class="lead">Zarathustra, known in Greek as Zoroaster, is the prophet to whom the Gathas — the oldest Avestan hymns — are attributed.</p>

## Who was he?

Almost everything about his life is uncertain. The language of the Gathas places him among the earliest Indo-Iranian speakers; linguists often suggest a range between roughly 1500 and 1000 BCE, though Zoroastrian tradition itself places him in the 6th century BCE. His homeland was probably in eastern Iran or Central Asia. No contemporary external records confirm his biography.

## Teaching

The Gathas present **Ahura Mazda** (the "Wise Lord") as the supreme deity. Humans choose between *asha* (truth, order, rightness) and *druj* (the lie, disorder, destruction), and that choice shapes the fate of the world. This ethical dualism is the core of what Zarathustra taught.

See [The Two Spirits](/cultures/iranian/stories/two-spirits/) for the foundational Gathic passage.

## The call

In the Gathas, Zarathustra describes himself as a *manthran* — a maker of sacred utterances — who received his revelation from Ahura Mazda. He speaks of opposition from those who practised the old sacrificial religion and of finding a protector in King Vishtaspa, whose patronage allowed the new teaching to spread.

## Later tradition

Texts written many centuries later (the *Denkard*, the *Arda Viraf Namag*) added legends: a miraculous birth foretold from conception, attempts on his life, visions of heaven and hell, and detailed ritual law. These are rich traditional accounts; they are not from the Gathas themselves and should be treated as later elaboration.
