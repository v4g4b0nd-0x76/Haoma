<p class="lead">Zoroastrian ideas shaped Iranian civilisation for over a millennium, from the Achaemenid empire through the Sasanian dynasty, and the community survived the Arab conquest to continue into the present day.</p>

## Achaemenid period (550–330 BCE)

The Achaemenid kings — Cyrus, Darius, Xerxes — used Ahura Mazda as their divine patron, as inscriptions at Persepolis and Naqsh-e Rostam make clear. Whether they were strictly Zoroastrian in Zarathustra's sense is debated; they also tolerated and even supported the religions of their subject peoples. The relationship between the royal religion and the Gathic tradition is not straightforward.

## Parthian period (247 BCE – 224 CE)

The Avestan texts were transmitted orally during this era. Zoroastrian priests (the *magi*) maintained the ritual tradition without the institutional backing that later came under the Sasanians. Little is known about how the religion was practised across this long period.

## Sasanian period (224–651 CE)

The Sasanian dynasty made Zoroastrianism the state religion. The Avesta was written down for the first time in a specially created script. A priestly hierarchy formed around the *mobadan mobad* (chief priest), and fire temples became visible institutions across the empire. This period also produced the main secondary texts — the *Bundahishn*, *Denkard*, *Arda Viraf Namag* — that expanded and systematised the earlier material.

## Arab conquest and after (651 CE onwards)

The defeat of the last Sasanian emperor at the Battle of Nihavand (642 CE) ended Zoroastrian political power in Iran. The community shrank over the following centuries as many converted. Some emigrated to India, where their descendants are known as **Parsis** and maintain the tradition today. A smaller community, the **Zartoshtis**, remained in Iran and continue to practise.

## The Avesta today

Only a fraction of the original Avestan corpus survives. The Yasna (including the Gathas), the Yashts (hymns to individual yazatas, including the Mihr Yasht), and the Vendidad form the core of what is extant. Much was lost before it could be written down.
